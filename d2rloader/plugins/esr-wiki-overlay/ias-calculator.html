Every class shares this cast animation, so this table is the same for all of them outside a form. Faster cast rate is capped at 500, and the rate it allows is already reached at 480. Next tier costs 9% more FCR and saves a frame. Floor is 7 frames at 200%.

How this is worked out. Gear speed is subject to diminishing returns before anything else happens: effective = 120 × IAS ÷ (120 + IAS), rounded toward zero. Negative attack speed goes through the same curve, so a penalty bites harder than its face value, and exactly −120 makes the game divide by zero and crash. Base attack speed and the weapon speed modifier are then added at full value, clamped so the resulting rate stays between 15% and 175%, or 250% while shapeshifted. The animation then runs at rate = floor(AnimationSpeed × (100 + total) ÷ 100) and takes ceil(256 × BaseFrames ÷ rate) − 1 frames. Casting is the same shape with its own ceiling: rate = min(120 × FCR ÷ (FCR + 120) + 100, 196), then animSpeed = rate × AnimationSpeed ÷ 100. The 196 ceiling is what the curve gives at 500 faster cast rate, the cap Eastern Sun sets, and the rounding already reaches it at 480, so nothing above 480 faster cast rate changes anything. Between −55 and −119 faster cast rate the game's own arithmetic wraps around and the cast becomes instant.

Whirlwind hits on your attack frame. The time between Whirlwind hits is the frame your normal attack would land on: the attack frame of your attack animation for the weapon in hand, played at your attack speed, floor(256 × AttackFrame ÷ floor(AnimationSpeed × rate ÷ 100)). The rate is the one every other attack uses, so all gear, skills, auras, buffs and slows count, diminished as usual, except that Whirlwind never takes the −30 sequence penalty. The animation comes from the weapon's own class, so two-handed swords are still timed on their one-handed animation. The first hit lands on the spin's first strike, a few frames after you start, and one lands every interval after that.

Whirlwind hits on your attack frame. The time between Whirlwind hits starts from the frame your normal attack would land on: the attack frame of your attack animation for the weapon in hand. Eastern Sun makes it 70% longer and plays it at a Whirlwind rate of its own, floor(256 × AttackFrame × 170 ÷ 100 ÷ floor(AnimationSpeed × rate ÷ 100)). That rate counts increased attack speed in full, diminished as usual, but base attack speed, the weapon speed modifier and slows at only 50% of their value. It stops at 300 whether you are shapeshifted or not, never takes the −30 sequence penalty, and no setup goes below 6 frames between hits. The animation comes from the weapon's own class, so two-handed swords are still timed on their one-handed animation. The first hit lands on the spin's first strike, a few frames after you start, and one lands every interval after that.

Wereforms. Shapeshifting does not give you the form's own frame count. The engine rescales the rate by the ratio of the form's frames to your human ones, so your breakpoints are the human ones for the weapon you are holding, give or take a frame of rounding, which the table works out exactly. What shapeshifting really buys is a higher ceiling: the speed clamp rises from 175 to 250, so attack speed keeps paying well past the point it stops mattering unshifted.

Strafe rewinds the animation after each arrow to releaseFrame × (100 − Param6) ÷ 100, so every shot after the first only replays the remainder. ESR uses Param6 = 15 throughout.

Sequences. Frenzy, charge-up strikes, Cleave and Mirrored Blades run fixed sequences compiled into the game rather than a plain animation, with an entry per weapon class. A sequence takes the −30 sequence penalty and counts its steps down to zero, which makes it one frame longer than a plain animation of the same length. Frenzy is 17 steps, striking on step 5 with the main hand and step 10 with the off hand. A charge-up is 12 steps striking on step 7; with a claw in each hand it becomes 16 steps, one claw striking on step 7 and the other on step 11. Cleave and Mirrored Blades play frames of the cast animation as an attack, 16 to 22 steps depending on the weapon, so attack speed drives them, not faster cast rate.

Who can dual wield. Only the Barbarian and the Assassin. The Assassin pairs claws and nothing else. The Barbarian pairs any one-handed weapon, and also the two-handed swords marked 1or2handed in weapons.txt — 39 of them, from Two-Handed Sword up to Colossus Blade. A sword held in one hand switches to its one-handed animation, which is usually faster than the two-handed one.

What the second weapon does. The game enables one weapon's stats at a time, so with a weapon in each hand each hand is worked out on its own weapon: that weapon's increased attack speed, base attack speed and speed modifier, plus everything else you wear. Frenzy and charge-up strikes average the two rates and only then apply the −30 and the clamp, so speed on one weapon counts at half weight while gloves and rings count in full. Whirlwind averages the two intervals instead, rounding up, and both weapons strike every hit. In the base game the second weapon has to find a different enemy; Eastern Sun lets both strike the same one.

Crossbows. Eastern Sun fixes every crossbow attack animation at 5 frames whatever the attack speed, and Mirrored Blades with a crossbow takes the same 5 frames for the whole attack. Items that add or remove crossbow attack frames move it directly. Whirlwind is not affected.

Frenzy. Eastern Sun's Frenzy frames bonus takes one frame off each of Frenzy's two swings for every point, two off the whole cycle, down to 3 frames, the shortest cycle in which both hits still land. A negative bonus adds two frames per point instead. Nothing else is affected, and the tables show Frenzy without the bonus.

Base frames and animation speeds are read from this build's animdata.txt; weapon speed modifiers from weapons.txt. Values will not match vanilla calculators where Eastern Sun has changed the underlying tables.
